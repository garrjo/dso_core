⚛ Physical Basis

The geophysical foundations of the Dynamic Stress Offset model

Core Hypothesis

The DSO model proposes that the Earth's inner core is not perfectly centered within the outer core, but instead exhibits a small positional offset that varies over time. This asymmetry in the core-mantle system creates differential stress patterns at the core-mantle boundary (CMB) that propagate upward through the mantle, ultimately influencing seismic stress distributions at plate boundaries and subduction zones.

🌍 Core Dynamics

The inner core, primarily composed of solid iron-nickel alloy, floats within the liquid outer core. Gravitational and electromagnetic forces maintain its position, but perturbations can cause small displacements.

⚙ Stress Transfer

Asymmetric pressure at the CMB creates differential heating and flow patterns in the lower mantle. These patterns influence convection cells that ultimately affect surface plate dynamics.

Key Assumptions & Limitations

  • ⚠
    Core Position Detectability Assumes inner core offset can be resolved from seismic travel time residuals with sufficient accuracy (< 0.5 km uncertainty)
  • ⚠
    Stress Coupling Efficiency Assumes efficient mechanical coupling between CMB pressure anomalies and lower mantle flow patterns
  • ⚠
    Temporal Correlation Assumes time lag between core displacement and surface stress expression is predictable (estimated 30-90 days)
  • ⚠
    Mantle Homogeneity Simplified treatment of mantle heterogeneity; actual stress propagation is more complex
  • ⚠
    Linear Superposition Assumes stress fields can be linearly superimposed; non-linear effects may be significant

Supporting Observations

🔬 Seismological Evidence

  • PKP travel time anomalies consistent with ~1 km inner core offset (Wang & Vidale, 2022)
  • Inner core super-rotation rate of 0.1-0.3°/year observed
  • Hemispherical anisotropy in inner core seismic velocities
  • Core-sensitive modes show temporal variations

🌍 Geodynamic Constraints

  • Electromagnetic torque calculations support core oscillation
  • Gravitational restoring force allows small displacements
  • Mantle convection patterns show long-wavelength structure
  • Plate velocity changes correlate with deep mantle anomalies
